# Greenblatt Lens

## Core Use

Use Greenblatt when comparing quality and cheapness across a list of companies or when a special situation needs a simple return-on-capital filter.

## Best For

- Watchlist ranking
- Quality-at-reasonable-price screening
- Peer comparison
- Special situations with clear economics

## Key Questions

- Does the company earn high returns on capital?
- Is the stock cheap relative to normalized earnings or FCF?
- Is cheapness due to temporary issues or structural decline?
- Does the company rank well against peers on both quality and valuation?

## Required Evidence

- Normalized EBIT / earnings
- Invested capital
- EV / EBIT or FCF yield
- Peer set
- One-cycle or normalized margin context

## Valuation Preference

- Earnings yield
- Return on capital screen
- Comparable multiples
- Normalized owner earnings cross-check

## Downgrade The Lens When

- Accounting capital is not economically meaningful.
- Reported earnings are distorted.
- A company requires a full specialist route, such as bank, insurer, REIT, or early-stage biotech.
