## Core Use

Use Jin Jiancheng when the central question is how to turn company analysis into an executable, risk-controlled plan:

- Is the business good enough?
- Is the financial report quality good enough?
- Is the price good enough?
- Is the investor's cash, cost basis, position size, and fallback plan good enough?

The lens is especially useful when the report needs buy / add / hold / trim / sell zones, tranche design, cash reserve discipline, or a plain-language stress test.

## Best For

- U.S. stocks, U.S. index ETFs, and globally important platform or technology companies
- High-quality companies with volatile prices
- Wide-moat companies where valuation must be translated into action zones
- Portfolio-aware buy / add / trim / sell decisions
- Cash reserve, cost basis, and position sizing discipline
- Situations where the user is tempted to chase, average down mechanically, use leverage, or over-concentrate

## Quick Filter

Run these questions before giving action guidance. Missing answers do not block company analysis, but they lower action confidence and should be marked as assumptions.

| # | Dimension | Question | Red Flag |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cash use | Is this capital long-term surplus capital? | Needed cash is being put at risk |
| 2 | Cash reserve | Does the investor still have liquid reserves after the trade? | No room to respond to drawdowns |
| 3 | Position size | Would this position be too large relative to net worth or portfolio? | Single-name concentration can impair judgment |
| 4 | Business quality | Is the company a first-tier business with a durable moat? | Falling price is confused with value |
| 5 | Financial quality | Do growth, margins, ROIC, FCF, and balance sheet support the thesis? | Narrative is stronger than cash evidence |
| 6 | Valuation range | Are bear / base / bull values triangulated by more than one method? | One metric drives the conclusion |
| 7 | Fundamental change | Is the decline temporary noise or evidence that the business has changed? | Averaging down into a broken thesis |
| 8 | Pre-commitment | Are add, hold, trim, and exit rules written before emotion rises? | Action depends on mood or headlines |

## Operating Framework

### 1. Do Not Predict; Prepare

The market cannot be forecast with useful precision. The controllable variables are cash, position size, cost basis, entry spacing, trim rules, and thesis-change triggers.

Application:

- Replace "will it go up or down?" with "what do we do if it rises, falls, or goes sideways?"
- Convert every conclusion into a plan for bear, base, and bull paths.
- Treat price levels as response triggers, not prophecies.

### 2. Defense Before Offense

Survival and optionality come before return maximization. Cash and low-risk assets are not dead money when they preserve the ability to act during dislocation.

Application:

- Do not let a single company, sector, factor, or leveraged instrument dominate the portfolio.
- Keep liquid reserves if the user asks for position-aware guidance.
- Downgrade action confidence when the analysis lacks information about cash needs, time horizon, or existing exposure.

### 3. Cash Flow Is the Steering Wheel

Investment resilience depends on current and future cash flow: business FCF, dividends, interest income, salary/business income, and other liquid resources.

Application:

- For the company: emphasize FCF conversion, FCF margin, net cash/debt, and reinvestment needs.
- For the investor action plan: separate "can buy because valuation is attractive" from "can buy because liquidity and position structure permit it."

### 4. Enterprise Value = Good Business + Good Financials + Good Price

The company analysis sequence is:

1. Good business: market position, moat, industry trend, management, culture, and durability.
2. Good financials: revenue quality, margin structure, EPS trend, ROE/ROIC, FCF conversion, and balance sheet health.
3. Good price: DCF or owner-earnings value, forward earnings/multiple range, PEG or growth-adjusted sanity check, P/FCF, historical comparison, peer comparison, and scenario range.

Do not treat a good company as a good investment until the third layer is also acceptable.

### 5. Fundamental Change Versus Temporary Shock

A drawdown is useful only if intrinsic value is intact. Separate business-model deterioration from external, temporary, or sentiment-driven pressure.

Application:

- Before adding after a drop, ask whether moat, unit economics, growth runway, margins, balance sheet, or regulation changed.
- If fundamentals deteriorated, lower intrinsic value before discussing cheaper price.
- If fundamentals remain intact, use the price decline as a candidate entry-zone signal, not an automatic buy signal.

### 6. Safety Margin Is Operational, Not Just Valuation

Margin of safety includes valuation discount, cash reserve, position size, cost basis, and execution spacing.

Application:

- Translate intrinsic value into price zones and tranche rules.
- Use wider tranche spacing when volatility, uncertainty, or valuation sensitivity is high.
- Avoid dense averaging down that consumes cash before the bear case has played out.

### 7. Pyramid Buying And Reverse Pyramid Selling

Buying should usually be pre-planned and staged. Selling can also be staged as price exceeds fair or optimistic value, especially when trimming is meant to recover capital, reduce cost basis, or rebalance exposure.

Application:

- For index-like broad exposures, staged buying can be tied to drawdown bands and valuation conditions.
- For single stocks, staged buying must first pass the "fundamentals intact" test.
- For trimming, use valuation excess, position size, thesis maturity, and opportunity cost rather than short-term price fear alone.

## Valuation Preference

Use this lens as an action and cross-check overlay, not as a replacement for the routed valuation model.

Preferred valuation stack:

- Primary routed model from the company type.
- DCF / owner earnings / FCF-based estimate when cash flows are visible.
- Forward EPS and reasonable multiple range for mature or platform companies with analyst-visible earnings paths.
- PEG or growth-adjusted sanity check for high-growth quality companies.
- P/FCF, historical range, peer comparison, and scenario analysis as triangulation.
- Reverse DCF when the market price embeds high expectations.

## Output Guidance

Default user-facing output should not explain the full Jin Jiancheng framework. Mention only the parts that changed the action conclusion.

Recommended compact language:

```text
Jin Jiancheng action overlay: used to translate valuation into cash reserve, position size, staged add/trim, and "fundamentals changed or only price changed" checks.
```

If a specific rule changes the conclusion, mention it directly:

- "Business quality is acceptable, but action confidence is capped because the plan lacks cash reserve and position-size information."
- "The key question is not whether the stock has fallen enough; it is whether the moat and FCF path are still intact."
- "The valuation range supports watch/add zones, but the tranche spacing should be wide because the bear case remains plausible."

## Downgrade The Lens When

- The task is a pure accounting audit, legal/tax plan, or institutional research comparison.
- The user needs a specialist valuation route where regulated assets dominate, such as banks, insurers, REITs, biotech rNPV, or commodity producers.
- The user asks for exact short-term price prediction.
- The company facts are stale or missing and current market data is required.
- The user has not provided portfolio context and the question demands personalized action.

## Hard Blocks

Block or downgrade action guidance when:

- The thesis depends on leverage, forced concentration, or cash the investor may need soon.
- The company fundamentals have deteriorated and the only positive evidence is a lower stock price.
- Valuation relies on one multiple without cash-flow, peer, scenario, or reverse-DCF cross-check.
- Current price, latest financial period, or material news is stale or missing.
- The user wants a direct personalized buy/sell order rather than conditional zones and decision rules.
